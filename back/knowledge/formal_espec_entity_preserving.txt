Formal Specification of Entity-Preserving RDB2RDF Views

This section summarizes the major concepts and results of a theory
of incremental RDB2RDF view maintenance, presented in detail
in the companion full paper [3]. The theory is the basis for the
incremental view maintenance tool described in Section 5.

3.1 Entity-Preserving RDB2RDF Views
An RDB2RDF view is a triple W = (𝑉 , S, M), where:
   • 𝑉 is the vocabulary of the target ontology;
   • S is the source relational schema; and
   • M is a set of mappings between 𝑉 and S, defined by transformation rules (TRs).
The semantics of an RDB2RDF view is defined in Section 3.3. A
view W = (𝑉 , S, M) satisfies the entity-preserving property iff:
   • the instances of the classes in 𝑉 correspond to tuples in
selected relations of S, which are called the pivot relations of W, so that distinct tuples from the same pivot relation
generate distinct class instances;
   • the values of datatype properties in 𝑉 of these instances are
given by (functions of) attribute values of the corresponding
tuples, or of related tuples;
   • the object properties in 𝑉 correspond to relationships between tuples of the pivot relations of S.

A transformation rule (TR) of W is an expreA transformation rule (TR) of W is an expression of the form
C(𝑥) ← Q(𝑥) or of the form P(𝑥,𝑦) ← Q(𝑥,𝑦), where C and P are class
and property names in 𝑉 , and Q(𝑥) and Q(𝑥,𝑦) are queries over S
whose target clauses contain one and two variables, respectively.
The problem of generating TRs is addressed in [5, 6] and is
outside of the scope of this work. Section 3.2 contains examples of
TRs for the RDB2RDF view used in the experiments.

Intuitively, a CTR Ψ maps tuples of a relation 𝑅 into RDF instances of a class 𝐶. It establishes a semantic correspondence between a pivot tuple 𝑟 and an RDF resource 𝑥, such that each pivot
tuple is associated with at most one RDF instance, and distinct
pivot tuples generate distinct RDF resources. Thus, the mapping
preserves the identity of relational entities in the RDB2RDF view.
An OTR Ψ defines object properties by relating RDF instances
generated from pivot tuples to RDF instances reached through
relational paths in the source schema. Hence, OTRs capture relationships between entities represented in the relational database.
There are two types of DTRs. A Local DTR has no relational path
in the body so that the datatype values are obtained directly from
attribute values of the pivot tuples. A Path DTR has a relational
path in the body, and the datatype values are obtained from tuples
reachable through the relational path from the pivot tuples.

whose tuples serve as the starting point (pivot tuples) for the construction of RDF triples. In particular, pivot tuples determine the
URI of the subject of the triples generated by Ψ as follows.

3.3 Hierarchical RDF-State Semantics of RDB2RDF Views
Let W = (𝑉 , S, M) be an an RDB2RDF view and 𝜎 be a database
state.
The state of W in 𝜎 is the set of quads (𝑠, 𝑝, 𝑜, 𝑔) generated by
the TRs in M when applied to 𝜎, where the fourth element 𝑔 is the
URI of the TR that generated the triple (𝑠, 𝑝, 𝑜). Intuitively, each
TR Ψ induces a named graph containing exactly the triples generated by Ψ, which provides a simple and operational mechanism for
provenance tracking and avoids ambiguity in the presence of duplicate triples generated by different TRs. The state of W is defined
compositionally in three hierarchical levels.
Let Ψ ∈ 𝑀 be a TR with tuple variables 𝑟1, . . . , 𝑟𝑛 associated with
relation schemes 𝑅1, . . . , 𝑅𝑛. For tuples 𝑝1 ∈ 𝑅1 (𝜎), . . . , 𝑝𝑛 ∈ 𝑅𝑛 (𝜎),
the application of Ψ under 𝜎 is the set of RDF triples produced by
evaluating Ψ with bindings 𝑟𝑖 = 𝑝𝑖, for 𝑖 = 1, . . . , 𝑛, over 𝜎, denoted
   Ψ[𝑟1 ← 𝑝1, . . . , 𝑟𝑛 ← 𝑝𝑛] (𝜎)
Assume that the pivot relation of Ψ is 𝑅∗. For a pivot tuple
𝑡 ∈ 𝑅∗(𝜎), the RDF contribution of 𝑡 under Ψ and 𝜎 is the set:
   𝑅𝐷𝐹_𝑆𝑡𝑎𝑡𝑒 [Ψ] (𝑡, 𝜎) =
